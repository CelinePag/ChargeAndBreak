\begin{abstract}
Battery-electric trucks must schedule charging jointly with the rest and break
periods that driving-hours regulation imposes, under stochastic travel times.
The strongest implementable policy we have for this problem is a
rolling-horizon look-ahead that solves its tail exactly --- and it costs 70\,s
per decision, which makes it a \emph{batch} policy rather than an operational
one. We ask how much of it survives amortisation into a network that decides in
0.5\,ms. Behaviour cloning reaches a 3.62\% gap to the hindsight optimum
against the look-ahead's 2.22\% and a myopic heuristic's 5.75\%; reinforcement
fine-tuning closes that to \textbf{2.93\%}, i.e.\ \textbf{80\% of the
heuristic-to-solver gap at $\sim$$5\times10^{4}$ less online compute}. Getting
there required repeatedly trusting closed-loop measurements over offline ones,
and the sharpest example is our own: a headline that the learned policy
\emph{beat} its teacher, with a reassuring $\pm0.05$ error bar over training
seeds, dissolved under a paired per-instance test into parity. We argue that in
sequential operational settings, offline predictive accuracy and seed-level
error bars are not merely weak proxies for policy quality --- they are
routinely \emph{anti-correlated} with it, and reporting them alone is how
learned policies come to be overclaimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem and motivation}
\label{sec:intro}

A battery-electric heavy truck on a long corridor faces two coupled schedules.
Charging is dictated by energy and by where stations are; rest and break periods
are dictated by EU driving-hours regulation~\citep{eu561}, on a corridor whose
station spacing is itself set by regulation~\citep{eu2023afir}. The two
interact: a mandatory break taken \emph{inside} a charging dwell is free,
because the two run in parallel, so \emph{when} to charge is inseparable from
\emph{when} to rest. Travel times are stochastic, so the schedule must be
decided online, stop by stop.

Our companion study benchmarks a family of policies on this problem --- a
myopic heuristic, static robust plans, a two-stage stochastic plan with
recourse, a rolling-horizon look-ahead, and a per-realisation hindsight oracle.
Here we take that benchmark as given and ask a narrower question about its best
implementable member.

\paragraph{Where the remaining headroom is.}
A one-at-a-time sweep of the look-ahead's parameters is uninformative in two of
its three dimensions. Raising the scenario count from 10 to 50 moves realised
duration by at most $0.4\%$; extending the horizon beyond one duty cycle
(24\,h) by at most $0.2\%$, although \emph{shortening} it below one duty cycle
costs up to $1.7\%$. The one live axis is \textbf{integrality}: solving the
look-ahead tail as a MIP rather than an LP relaxation roughly halves the gap to
the hindsight optimum (e.g.\ $4.3\%\!\to\!2.1\%$ on short routes without
windows, $6.8\%\!\to\!2.2\%$ on medium routes with tight ones) and costs an
order of magnitude more time per decision.

\paragraph{The gap this paper attacks.}
Exactness is therefore worth about two points of optimality and is now the
benchmark's standard configuration --- but at 45--90\,s per decision and
0.6--4.1\,h of wall clock per route it is affordable only as an offline
campaign. It cannot be re-evaluated continuously in a cab, run across a fleet
by a dispatcher, or deployed without a commercial solver licence. We therefore
ask: \emph{how much of an exact look-ahead survives compression into a policy
that decides in milliseconds?} In Powell's taxonomy~\citep{powell2022rlso} this
converts a direct look-ahead into a policy-function approximation; in the
ML-for-combinatorial-optimisation framing~\citep{bengio2021ml4co} it is
amortised optimisation~\citep{dumouchelle2022neur2sp,nair2020neuraldiving}.
Unlike neural construction heuristics for routing~\citep{kool2019attention} the
stop sequence is fixed and the learned object is the operational schedule; the
closest application is safe reinforcement learning for dynamic electric vehicle
routing~\citep{basso2022dsevrp}.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Decision process.}
At stop $i$ the state comprises state of charge, the regulatory clocks
(consecutive driving, shift driving, shift work, and the 15\,h daily
\emph{spread}), the split-break flag, budgets consumed, and arrival time; plus
the road ahead --- the next $K$ nodes (leg duration and energy, node type,
queue delay, time-window slack \emph{relative to now}) and aggregates over the
whole remaining route. With $K=20$ this is 143 features. The action is the
joint discrete choice over charging, break type and rest type, of which 12
combinations occur in the data, together with a charge duration $\tau_c$. The
reward is the negative increment of the benchmark's own objective (duration
plus $\beta$ per missed window), so training and evaluation optimise the same
quantity.

\paragraph{Policy and constraint layers.}
A two-layer MLP ($143\to128\to128$; 36{,}621 parameters) with masked logits over
the 12 actions and a softplus charge duration. Two constraint layers wrap it,
both reusing the benchmark's own feasibility checks so the learned policy obeys
exactly the rules every other policy obeys. \emph{Masking} sets structurally
impossible actions to $-\infty$ before the softmax. \emph{Forcing} restricts
the choice set when a one-step check declares a charge, break or rest mandatory
--- the network still chooses freely \emph{among} compliant actions, so the
rules constrain rather than decide. Charge duration is clamped below by the
energy needed to reach the next station at the $0.5$-quantile of consumption,
the same rule the teacher uses. \textbf{No solver runs at deployment.}

\paragraph{Cloning, then fine-tuning.}
767 exact-tail look-ahead runs over a $3\times3\times4$ grid of route length,
customer density and window class supply \textbf{74{,}380} labelled decisions;
instances are split by seed into 530 train / 129 validation / 108 test.
Behaviour cloning gives the initial policy; a round of
DAgger~\citep{ross2011dagger} on states the clone itself visits improved
validation but did not transfer to the held-out set, so it is not used here.
Because imitation is bounded by its teacher by construction, we fine-tune with
PPO on the realised objective,
initialised from the clone, with a value head on the shared trunk and a KL
penalty back to the frozen clone. Training episodes draw \emph{fresh} travel-time
realisations --- each instance ships one recorded realisation, which evaluation
uses, and training on it repeatedly would learn a plan rather than a policy.
Only the discrete head is fine-tuned. A halt (any regulatory breach ends the
route) is charged twice an estimate of what completing would have cost;
Section~\ref{sec:exp} shows that this shaping choice, not the RL algorithm, is
what governs reliability.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

Every policy runs in the same simulator, over the same recorded realisations,
scored by the same metrics. A run that breaches a regulatory limit \emph{halts}
at that stop and has no duration, so halts are counted, never averaged. We
report the gap to the per-realisation hindsight oracle --- an absolute yardstick
--- alongside paired per-instance differences from the teacher. Three
independently trained seeds are averaged \emph{per instance} first, so the unit
of analysis is the instance rather than the (instance, seed) pair.

\paragraph{Main result.}
Table~\ref{tab:main} and Figure~\ref{fig:money} give the headline. Cloning the
look-ahead reaches a $3.62\%$ oracle gap; PPO fine-tuning closes this to
$\mathbf{2.93\%}$ against the look-ahead's $2.22\%$, i.e.\ \textbf{80\% of the
distance between the myopic heuristic and the exact solver}, while deciding in
$0.51$\,ms and requiring no solver at all.

\paragraph{But it does not beat its teacher, and the error bar said it did.}
Across three seeds the fine-tuned policy's median duration difference was
$-0.24\pm0.05\%$ --- negative on every seed, with a tight spread. Read
literally, the student beat a 70-second solver. It does not. That $\pm$
measures \emph{seed reproducibility}, not whether the policy is better: seed
spread ($0.05$) is an order of magnitude smaller than instance-to-instance
spread. A paired per-instance test on validation puts the 95\% bootstrap CI of
the median at $[-0.17,+0.05]$\,h with the policy ahead on 54\% of instances
(exact sign test $p=0.19$); on the test set it is ahead on 45\%
($p=0.88$). \textbf{The honest claim is parity, not superiority}, and detecting
a 54\% win rate would need on the order of a thousand instances against the 237
uncontaminated ones we have. We report this at length because the mistake was
ours, it survived three seeds, and nothing in the offline metrics would have
caught it.

\paragraph{Ablations: the offline metric prefers the worse policy.}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} pairs each design decision's supervised metric with
its realised effect. Inverse-frequency class weighting is the clearest case.
The decision distribution is dominated by ``keep driving'' ($87.9\%$), so
weighting rare classes up is the textbook response, and by its own measure it
works --- balanced accuracy rises from $0.299$ to $0.477$. But it places the
majority class at weight $0.035$ against $5.3$ for the rarest, so predicting a
\emph{rest} when the truth is \emph{drive on} becomes nearly free in the loss
while costing 9--11\,h of route time in reality; realised duration degrades
sixfold. The loss had decorrelated from the objective, and no offline metric
revealed it.

\paragraph{What made reinforcement learning work.}
A first PPO run improved duration but raised halts by 70\%, and 27 of its 29
failures breached the 15\,h daily-spread ceiling --- a limit the policy
\emph{could not observe}, because the spread clock was absent from the state.
Two changes followed: adding it (and its headroom) as features, and replacing a
fixed halt surcharge with one proportional to the estimated cost of completing.
Rerunning with one change at a time separates their roles cleanly
(Table~\ref{tab:rlablation}): the feature supplies the quality, the penalty
supplies the reliability, and with the original additive penalty reinforcement
learning does not reduce halts at all.

\paragraph{Behavioural fidelity, and what is still missing.}
Figure~\ref{fig:schedule} shows a realised schedule against the teacher's: rest
placement, charging episodes and the state-of-charge trajectory are essentially
superimposed, and both policies hide almost every mandatory break inside a
charging dwell --- the coupling that makes this problem non-separable. The
learned policy charges $0.9\%$ \emph{less} than the teacher and runs the pack to
the same depth, so it has learned that charge is time rather than merely that
empty batteries are bad. Time windows remain the deficiency (118 misses against
53), which is why the penalised gap closes less than the duration gap; halts
also do not transfer consistently, being lower than the clone's on validation
and higher on test.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:disc}

An exact-tail look-ahead can be compressed into a 36k-parameter network that
decides $\sim$$5\times10^{4}$ times faster and gives up about seven tenths of a
point of oracle gap --- the difference between a policy that can be run as an
offline study and one that can be run in a vehicle.

The transferable lesson is methodological, and it has two halves. The first is
familiar: offline predictive accuracy repeatedly preferred the worse policy,
because in sequential operational problems the cost of an error is wildly
non-uniform across the action space --- a misplaced daily rest costs nine hours,
a misplaced charging decision costs minutes --- and any loss that treats them
comparably has stopped measuring the objective. The second is less often stated.
Having moved to closed-loop cost, we still overclaimed, because we reported
variability across training seeds. That quantity is easy to compute, looks like
an error bar, and answers the wrong question; the instance-level uncertainty
that decides whether one policy beats another was an order of magnitude larger.
We would encourage reporting realised closed-loop cost against an absolute
yardstick, with paired per-instance tests, and treating seed spread as a
reproducibility diagnostic rather than a confidence interval.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Single vehicle; synthetic travel-time uncertainty from a fitted shifted
lognormal; three training seeds; the test set was evaluated twice, once for an
earlier feature set and once for the final system, with all tuning confined to
validation. Time windows are the standing deficiency and reinforcement learning
did not repair them despite their being priced in the reward, which suggests
the signal is too weak relative to duration. Extending to a fleet sharing
charging infrastructure is the natural next step, and turns a single-agent
problem into a multi-agent one.
