ChargeAndBreak ML Status

Learned policies for an electric truck's charging, break and rest decisions under EU driving-time rules, trained to imitate the look-ahead MILP (the LA). This page records what we tried, what worked, what it means for the trucking problem, and what runs next. It is updated as results come in.

Where we are

Base case
The students match the LA. Best: PyTorch model at +0.17 ± 0.30 % route duration vs LA (median −0.07 %), trees +0.48 ± 0.35 %, 0 rule violations in 375 runs each. A decision takes 3 ms instead of 71 s.
Generalization
Works between charger powers and battery sizes it has seen. Fails below that range (150 kW chargers) and when charger power varies along a route: every student loses 5 to 9 pp, the PyTorch model 22.5 pp, while the LA loses nothing.
Beating the LA
Three attempts (rollout, policy iteration, MILP end game), all negative. The LA's worst routes are an extra daily rest near the end of long routes; none of the fixes removed it.
Direction
On mixed-power routes, asking the LA where the student goes (DAgger) is the most LA-efficient fix: 1,781 LA calls do as well as 121 whole routes (8,246 decisions). But both stall: against the LA on all 31 paired test routes the best students still lose about 2 pp more (DAgger +2.1 ± 1.5; no mixed data +4.5). More data or a second DAgger round does not shrink it. Built-in charger structure (direction B) adds nothing measurable.

The setup in brief

The problem

A battery truck drives a fixed route of stops: chargers, laybys, customers with time windows. At each stop it decides whether to charge, for how long, and whether to take a 45-min break (or a 15 + 30 split) or a daily rest, under EU Regulation 561/2006 driving and duty limits. Travel times are uncertain. The objective is arrival time.

Teacher, reference, student

LA: at every stop, a MILP over the next 24 h scored on 25 travel-time scenarios. Oracle: the same MILP with hindsight, the lower bound. Student: learns the LA's cost of every action at every stop (regret in hours) and picks the cheapest legal action.

The shield

Legality is never learned. The simulator's own rules enumerate legal actions; a spread-room check and a drive guard at the 0.99 travel-time quantile filter them. All headline numbers use the full shield.

How models are judged

Routes split by seed: 1–19 to train, 20–21 to choose models, 22–25 (125 routes) to test, once. "vs LA" is the mean % difference in route duration, paired by route. "Gap" is % above the hindsight oracle.

What it means for trucking

  • On networks like the ones it learned from, a cheap learned policy makes charging and break decisions as good as the stochastic look-ahead MILP, and it never breaks the driving rules because the rules are enforced outside the model.
  • The look-ahead itself stays about 2–3 % above the hindsight optimum, and its worst cases are one daily rest too many near the end of long routes. That is a planning-horizon effect, and none of our learning fixes removed it.
  • When charger power varies along a route, the optimization model adapts at no cost: it charges where chargers are fast. Learned policies trained on uniform networks do not, and lose 5–9 % of route time. Real networks will be upgraded gradually, so mixed power is the realistic case.
  • Varying charger spacing is harmless. A slower charger network than any seen in training is not.
  • The students miss customer time windows about twice as often as the LA (112–120 vs 57 over 737 visits).
  • 70 s per stop is acceptable for a driver. The case for learning has to come from what a learned policy can do structurally, not from speed.

Direction B and next steps

Goal: a network whose structure compares the chargers ahead (power, distance, queue, charge on arrival) with the charger the truck is at, so that the decision "skip this slow charger and charge at the fast one" can come from structure, not from LA runs on mixed routes.

  • B0 (done): the current PyTorch recipe on every physics value. Seeing 150–1000 kW chargers removes the collapse but not the comparison gap, so structure is needed.
  • New inputs (done, being re-extracted): the same seven numbers for the charger here and each of the next six, including hours to charge to full at that charger. Hours-to-full varies within uniform routes, so its effect can be learned from them.
  • B1 (queued): the charger-comparison network. One small sub-network values every charger; charging here is compared with the best reachable charger ahead. Three variants (no structure, structure, structure with this charger's speed also in the base network), chosen on 16 new mixed-power validation routes, then tested once.
  • Compare B1 with the pilot (LA data from 48 mixed routes) and with DAgger on the same routes, at equal LA time.
  • Model search across families and settings, chosen on validation routes, tested once.
  • Open: time-window misses (twice the LA's); the 0.4 pp cost of training on every physics value.

Reading the numbers

  • Differences under about 1 pp on 30–50 routes are within noise; on the 125 base-case routes, under about 0.5 pp.
  • The LA is not deterministic: the same route and travel times give durations that differ by 0.35 % (median) and up to 18 %.
  • The mixed-power comparison with the LA rests on 8 routes; the student-only comparison on 32.
  • Since 1 Oct, models are chosen on validation routes and tested once. Some earlier tables chose feature sets on the test routes and are slightly optimistic.
